1. **The model treats ancillary revenue as costless, invalidating contribution, break-even, and runway.**  
   **Quotes:** “Full-audit upgrade… $25 flat”; “the worker… shares the [data-licensing] revenue”; “Category certification — A paid exam”; and “Contribution at 7.4% blend $2.26/task.”  
   **What is wrong:** The $2.26 calculation credits the entire extra 1.4% take without any corresponding costs:  
   \[
   61\times7.4\%-1.90-0.35=2.264
   \]
   But full audits add roughly \(90\%\times \$19=\$17.10\) per upgrade; licensing owes workers a revenue share; exams require assessment. Those costs are absent. Consequently the claimed \(2.26\times2,400\approx \$5,424\) month-12 contribution, 1,440-task break-even, month-11 profitability, and cash curve do not follow.  
   **Minimal fix:** Model each ancillary line with volume, price, direct cost, and revenue share; then recompute contribution, break-even, cash, and tranche size.

2. **The plan contradicts itself about who pays the 6% settlement fee.**  
   **Quotes:** “Settlement fee… taken from the poster’s escrow at payout”; “The two fee columns are what the worker pays”; and the ledger has the poster paying “the $61 ticket plus the 1% triage fee” while the worker receives “$57.34 released from escrow” on a $61 ticket.  
   **What is wrong:** The ledger makes the worker pay:  
   \[
   \$61-\$57.34=\$3.66=6\%\times \$61.
   \]
   The poster does not pay that fee on top. This changes the advertised buyer price, worker retention economics, and every “worker keeps” comparison.  
   **Minimal fix:** Choose one incidence. Either call it a worker fee, or charge the poster \(61+3.66+0.61=\$65.27\) and redo the ledger and buyer-cost metrics.

3. **The load-bearing audit cost is presented as measured when it is only an assumption.**  
   **Quotes:** “$14–$38 per audit, $19 median”; “$19 is one human audit: 20 minutes × $57/hour”; and “not a number the team picked.”  
   **What is wrong:** No completed Foreguild audits, timing sample, reviewer wage observation, or median calculation is supplied. Multiplying an assumed duration by an assumed hourly rate does not produce a measured median. This number determines the $37.50 ticket floor and most unit economics.  
   **Minimal fix:** Label $19 as an assumption and provide sensitivity cases, or run and publish a timed audit sample before using “median” or “measured.”

4. **The governance eligibility rule contradicts itself and the proposed fee market violates it.**  
   **Quotes:** “Everything above 2% goes to a market”; “gate futarchy to decisions where a wrong call costs under 5% of monthly revenue”; and “A two-point fee move is a third of take, so it goes to a market.”  
   **What is wrong:** A decision cannot simultaneously be routed to futarchy because its impact exceeds 2% and excluded because its impact exceeds 5%. The principal fee decision moves roughly 33% of take, so the stated rules give opposite answers. Separately, making the fee market “a branch of whichever parent is carrying traders” does not create a valid conditional decision market if that parent is an unrelated election or news event.  
   **Minimal fix:** Publish one non-overlapping impact rule, and create a fee-decision parent whose branches are the actual fee alternatives; do not attach governance branches to unrelated hot markets.

5. **The claimed audit-cost conservatism uses the wrong lane share.**  
   **Quotes:** The mix is “60%… average ~$60,” “30%… average ~$12,” and “10% above $150”; later, “the mix implies an audit on 60% of them… about $1.14,” overstating cost “by roughly $0.76.”  
   **What is wrong:** Both the 60% at about $60 and the 10% above $150 are in the audited lane, so the audited share is 70%, not 60%:  
   \[
   70\%\times10\%\times\$19=\$1.33,
   \]
   not  
   \[
   60\%\times10\%\times\$19=\$1.14.
   \]
   Therefore the stated overstatement is \(\$1.90-\$1.33=\$0.57\), not $0.76.  
   **Minimal fix:** Change the audited share to 70%, the implied cost to $1.33, and the conservatism claim to $0.57 per task.

6. **The dated “falsifiable” predictions are not falsifiable at their stated targets.**  
   **Quotes:** “40 tasks settled… falsified if fewer than 20”; “≥70%… falsified if under 50%”; “≥85%… falsified if under 75%”; “≥35%… falsified if under 20%”; “≤1.6×… falsified if above 2.5×.”  
   **What is wrong:** Outcomes between target and failure threshold—such as 30 tasks, 60% deterministic settlement, or 25% repeat posters—both miss the prediction and avoid falsification. That defeats the claimed accountability and weakens the month-5 tranche gate.  
   **Minimal fix:** Define failure as failure to meet each stated target, or relabel the lower numbers as kill criteria and report target attainment separately.
